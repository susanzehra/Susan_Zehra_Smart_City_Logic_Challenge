Quick review

What you need before starting

CNF

A conjunction of clauses; each clause is a disjunction of literals.

(¬P ∨ Q) ∧ (R ∨ S)

Resolution

Combine clauses containing complementary literals and remove that pair.

(P ∨ Q), (¬P ∨ R) ⟹ (Q ∨ R)

Refutation

To prove KB ⊨ α, add ¬α. Deriving the empty clause means a contradiction was found.

KB ∧ ¬α ⟹ □

First-order logic

Predicates describe properties and relationships among objects in a domain.

Serves(robot1, stationA)

Quantifiers

∀ means “for every.” ∃ means “there exists at least one.” Scope and order matter.

∀x ∃y Serves(x,y)

Translation tip

Universal categories usually use implication; existential claims usually use conjunction.

∀x(Robot(x) → Safe(x))

Mission 3 · Resolution detective

Follow the evidence to a contradiction

The city database contains these clauses:

C₁: A ∨ BC₂: ¬AC₃: ¬B ∨ CC₄: ¬C
  1. Resolve C₁ and C₂.
  2. Resolve that result with C₃.
  3. Resolve that result with C₄.
  4. What does the final result mean?
Need a hint?

Resolve complementary pairs in this order: A with ¬A, then B with ¬B, and finally C with ¬C.

Mission 6 · Policy translator

Translate real policies into FOL

Domain: all city objects. Predicates: Robot(x), Authorized(x), Station(y), Serves(x,y).
1. Every authorized robot serves at least one station.
2. Some station is served by every robot.
Need a hint?

“Every robot has some station” has the pattern ∀x∃y. “There is one station for every robot” has the pattern ∃y∀x.
